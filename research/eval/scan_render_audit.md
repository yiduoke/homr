# What the scans and renders show

Released weights, layout1, 17 November 2026. We compared all 29 pairs visually. We read a matched passage in 23 pieces and the opening systems in six. There are usable numerical scores for 28. The sampled bars emphasize staff-assignment differences and scan/render disagreements. They show individual failure modes and cannot estimate how often each occurs.

## Findings for the next experiment

- **Report pitch both ways.** In *Waldstein* m. 145 the scan’s upper staff uses bass clef for a running figure that the render assigns to the lower staff. The scan recovers all 19 pitches, but only one on its reference staff. Across the piece, pooling staves raises scan pitch F1 from 94.2 to 99.5. In *Hammerklavier* m. 385 the corresponding counts are 4 and 18 of 18, and whole-piece F1 rises from 89.5 to 95.9. Use pooled pitch inventory to track the 97% pitch goal alongside staff-sensitive pitch and pitch+onset+duration. Pooling can also forgive a model’s genuine staff mistake.
- **Follow the Rhapsody repetition.** In Hungarian Rhapsody No. 6 the render scores 86.2 pitch F1 and the scan 91.8. Rendered page 8 shows an ascending and descending run. Its fourth output measure has 615 pitched notes, including 414 consecutive G5s. Of 62 reference notes in the aligned m. 93, 37 match. The remaining 578 predictions account for 64% of the render’s 902 unmatched predictions. The repetition is already present in the per-page XML. Replay the crop with the existing repetition guard and check where the music resumes. Recovery on this rendered case is unmeasured. [Rendered page](pages/liszt-hr-6-rendered/page-08.png) · [Per-page XML](testset/layout1/liszt-hr-6-rendered/page-xml/page-08.musicxml)
- **Test spatial resolution under a fixed training recipe.** Compare the current 256×1280 staff input and 16×80 CNN grid with a finer spatial grid. Log the actual input and output shapes and hold the training examples, decoder, seeds and evaluation pipeline fixed. Inspect held-out real scans for octave-span and clef errors, missing pitches in dense runs, and chord substitutions. The three Schott Liszt scan scores are confounded by staff pairing. The audit identifies cases to evaluate, while the controlled comparison is needed to establish a resolution effect.

## Render and reference XML corrections

**First checks.** Trace the octave spans in Chopin’s *Winter Wind* m. 20 and Schubert’s Impromptu D. 899 No. 4 m. 64. Read the printed scan through the first note after each bracket. Compare the covered notes and their sounding octaves with the source MusicXML, the rendered page and homr’s output for each image. Both renders recovered the full pitch inventory in these bars (33/33 and 14/14). The scan discrepancies motivate checking the reference against the print as well as checking the render against its source. The cause of each octave discrepancy is open.

**How to correct an affected pair.** If the rendered page changes what its source encodes, fix the rendering input or renderer, regenerate the affected pages and rerun recognition on them. Scriabin’s stray 47-note part was one confirmed case and has already been removed from the render. If the reference transcribes a pitch or octave span incorrectly, record the edition, page, measure and evidence, preserve the original XML, and rescore the affected outputs against the corrected reference. Regenerate the render too if the correction changes the visible music. Use the same corrected benchmark version for every checkpoint. A documented edition difference, such as the staff allocation in *Waldstein* m. 145, can be retained. If the scan needs an edition-specific reference, label it separately.

The 414 consecutive G5s in Hungarian Rhapsody No. 6 occur in homr’s per-page output. The rendered page shows a changing run. Follow that case through recognition. The reference checks can proceed alongside the controlled resolution experiment.

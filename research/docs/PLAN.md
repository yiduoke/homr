# Piano OMR plan — next runs and the path to 97% per piece

**Goal.** Reach 97% **staff-sensitive sounding-pitch F1 on each real piece**. Each visible notehead must match the pitch, octave, aligned bar and staff on which the edition prints it, including when a voice or beam crosses staves. Report each printed staff’s F1 alongside the piece score so a strong upper staff cannot hide a weak lower one. Across 28 pieces the released model averages 90.25% staff-sensitive pitch, 75.63% with onset and 73.69% with onset and duration. Five pieces reach 97%. Pooling the staves gives 91.99% pitch and six pieces at 97%. Keep pooling as a diagnostic. [Released scores](out/testset/RES-FINAL-table-2026-11-17.md) · [Scoring definitions](res_wt/validation/note_scoring.md)

**Reference staff.** Verify discrepancies against the printed edition. In the Waldstein first movement, bar 145, the scan places a figure on the upper staff in bass clef while the reference rendering places it below. Record a verified, edition-specific staff correction before counting such a case as a model error. Where staff counts differ, the existing scorer can remap by mean pitch. Mark those results provisional until the correspondence is verified from the page, starting with Petrushka. [Scan and render audit](eval_run1/scan_render_audit.md)

**Why this order.** On 72 held-out passages the released model reads pitch almost equally well on scans and their rendered twins (99.1% and 99.6%). Requiring the right rhythm token widens the gap to 71.7% versus 99.1%. These passages come from just two works, and the renderer prints triplet numbers that the Schubert edition omits. Separately, a nine-piece timing audit attributed 64% of pitch-matched timing errors to tuplets or their downstream effects. That gives us a reason to test scan exposure first and explicit rhythm labels second. A missing measure, a dropped notehead and a correctly detected note at the wrong pitch each need a different fix. [Pilot v2, §7](scan_pairs_virtuoso/PILOT_SPEC.md) · [Timing audit](eval_run1/timing_audit.md)

## 1. Near term — real scans versus rendered twins

## 2. Data preparation and training — whole-bar rests and tuplets

1. The full-measure-rest audit found zero typeless rests in 56,441 inspected source XML files from the main mixture. Keep its current rest vocabulary. Apply the verified converter fix to incoming ASAP sources and test the MusicXML writer on rest-only bars that overrun their meter. Preserve genuine whole-bar rests, pickups, typed eighth rests and partial-voice rests. Use the same corrected labels in both tuplet arms. [Bar-rest audit](barrest_audit/REPORT.md)
2. Compare the current rhythm encoding with explicit tuplet groups on identical passages. Record the voice, group boundaries, written note/rest values and dots, exact ratio, and whether a numeral or bracket is printed. Include mixed values, rests, nested groups, and passages where the edition stops numbering successive groups. Recover exact ratios from source and verify against the print before using previously rounded labels. An unmarked group can have correct timing and leave its number invisible in MusicXML.
3. Check that decoding reproduces the printed group and computes the correct onsets and durations. Score boundaries, ratios, invented marks and full-note F1 separately on marked groups, implied groups and ordinary compound meter. If a staff crop leaves the interpretation ambiguous, test an ordered pass over adjacent systems with the page images still decoded in parallel.

## 3. Training and analysis — give staff-position pitch a direct test

1. **Staff position gets the first training slot.** Christian confirmed upstream tried predicting line/space position and saw no accuracy gain. Retest it on our scan mix. Compare written note names with a diatonic line/space index, keeping the images, printed accidentals, octave shifts, update budget and seeds identical. Decode each index using the clef on the staff carrying the notehead. Score written and sounding pitch on ledger lines, clef changes and cross-staff passages. Score once more with verified clefs to measure the cost of a misread clef. [Christian’s reply](https://github.com/liebharc/homr/discussions/226)
2. **Chords are the next independent comparison.** Encode the shared rhythm once and retain every notehead, accidental and tie. Keep simultaneous voices with different durations distinct. A beam can span several successive chords. Measure missing chord members, repeated-note recall and full-note F1 against the same control.
3. **Free-form meter follows chords.** Compare the existing whole-signature labels with separate numerator and denominator digits. Preserve printed common time and cut time as distinct symbols. Reuse the prepared meter corpus in both arms. Check validation works against the entire training mixture. Score exact printed signatures, changes and invented signatures on real editions. [Prepared meter corpus](box_scripts/run4_meters/README.md)

## Longer term — close the gap on the weakest pieces

1. **Target the largest verified loss.** Use the round 1 replays to choose a pitch experiment. For dropped attacks or chord members, train on verified repeated-note and dense-chord passages and report note recall and extra notes. For octave errors, collect 8va/8vb starts, continuations and stops across systems and pages, plus unbracketed high and low passages. Test the extra exposure separately and report false 8vb as well as recovered notes. Record two-octave marks wherever the print can be verified. [Octave examples](octave_checks/REPORT.md)
2. **Test architecture where the evidence points.** If small printed details remain unreadable on well-aligned crops, compare native higher-detail scans or renders with downsampled copies of the identical passages. If clear, correctly cropped notes are still skipped, test decoder capacity separately at fixed resolution and data. Use supervised training, retain visible notation in MusicXML, and check any slower candidate on complete pieces. [Resolution follow-ups](eval_run1/RESOLUTION_EVAL.md)
3. **Test transfer to fresh editions.** Use independent development works to select changes. Combine successful changes and evaluate one release candidate end to end on the frozen 28 and on previously unseen editions. Verify Petrushka’s alignment against its print. Track the lowest-scoring piece and how many reach 97% alongside the mean, staff assignment and timing. These measures distinguish improved reading from improvements confined to one edition.

**Optional two-week pitch sprint.** If funded at the proposed $200 Vast.ai cap, use week one for the scan pilot and the replays with verified staff pairs, bars and octave spans. In week two, target the largest verified source of pitch loss on independent development works. Judge progress by the worst piece and the number reaching 97%. Favor reproducible real-scan accuracy gains even if inference takes several minutes.

**Reporting.** Rank candidates and count pieces reaching 97% using staff-sensitive pitch F1. For both seeds, show each staff, the combined piece score, and how many pieces reach 97% on every staff. Include staff-sensitive pitch+onset and full-note F1. Show pooled pitch alongside them to investigate differences in staff assignment. For a release candidate, run the repository benchmarks and inspect every regressing page.
